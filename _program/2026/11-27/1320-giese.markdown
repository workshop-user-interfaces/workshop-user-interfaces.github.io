Open-source software thrives on its community of users and contributors, but it's precisely this community that relies on documentation and getting-started guides to be able to fully realize a tool’s potential. Missing, outdated, or poorly organized documentation is considered one of the main hurdles for new users and contributors in open-source projects. Especially with exploratory visualization tools like Glyphspace, where the software’s complexity makes its operation difficult to figure out intuitively, learnability is a crucial factor for acceptance and sustained use. At the same time, open-source projects typically have only limited human resources, raising the question of what form of support would provide the greatest benefit to different target groups while remaining feasible in teams of effort.
